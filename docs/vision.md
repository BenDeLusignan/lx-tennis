# LX Tennis: vision

This is the single source of truth for what we're building and the rules we build by.
Read it before you plan, build or review anything in this repo.

## How to read it

Every rule here is labelled with who owns it, because that decides who you talk to about it:

- **Ben's decisions.** Ben made these calls. Follow them. If a request conflicts with one, raise it with Ben. Don't quietly deviate.
- **Team conventions.** The way the team builds today. Follow them too, but they aren't Ben's calls: the team can change one through review (raise it with your teammate, not Ben), and updates this doc in the same change.

## What and why (Ben's decision)

A site where Ben and his tennis friends record the racquets and string setups they've tested, with each person's own scores and reviews side by side.

Why: published online reviews often don't match how the group feels a frame or string plays. This is the group's own record, in their own words and scores.

## Who it's for (Ben's decision)

- **Reviewers:** Ben, Michal and Otavio (more over time). They log in to enter setups and reviews.
- **Visitors:** anyone else. They can browse and read everything without logging in, but can't enter or change anything.
- Used on a phone, at the court.
- Ben, Michal and Otavio are men; use he/him for them in docs and copy.

## Core flow (Ben's decision)

**Racquets → a racquet (e.g. Yonex EZONE 98) → Ben's, Michal's and Otavio's reviews.**

The Racquets step has its own page, `/racquets`. The landing page is the hub that leads to it (see "Landing page is a hub" under "Changes Ben wants next").

## Product rules (Ben's decisions)

1. **A score belongs to a setup, not to a racquet or a string on its own.** A setup is:
   - the racquet;
   - a string, gauge and tension for the **Mains**, and a string, gauge and tension for the **Crosses**:
     - **full bed**: one string and gauge throughout; the mains and crosses tensions can be the same or different;
     - **hybrid**: any strings, gauges and tensions in the mains and crosses;
   - the date strung. Restringing the same combination on a new date makes a new setup.

   **Gauge is in millimetres** (e.g. 1.25 mm), the unit every brand prints.

   When entering a setup, the form always shows a **Mains:** area and a **Crosses:** area, for a full bed as well as a hybrid. A full bed takes one string and gauge (entered in Mains, with Crosses saying it's the same) plus a tension in each area. Choosing "Hybrid" reveals the crosses' own string and gauge, so each area has its own string, gauge and tension.
2. **One review per person per setup, and each setup exists once.** Entering a setup that already exists (same racquet, strings, gauges, tensions and date strung) is refused. A reviewer who hasn't reviewed it is pointed to the existing one to add their review; one who has is offered to update their review instead. That way everyone's scores for the same stringing sit side by side, so agreements and disagreements are easy to see.
3. **Side by side.** All reviewers' scores for a setup appear together, on the same criteria. On a racquet's page, each setup is a card:
   - the setup on top: full bed or hybrid, string(s), gauge(s), tensions in lb and kg, date strung;
   - then a score grid with the criteria as rows and the reviewers as columns, so comparing everyone on one criterion means reading across one row; only a reviewer who has reviewed that setup gets a column, and one who hasn't has no column at all (no empty slot), so a score is never made up (rule 9). The grid ends with a **Total** row: each reviewer's five scores added up, out of 50, worked out when the grid is drawn and never stored (a setup with one reviewer shows it too, so the rule is the same everywhere);
   - then the written reviews.
4. **Scoring criteria:** power, control, comfort, spin and feel. Every review scores all five: a reviewer can't leave a criterion blank. A written review alongside the scores is optional.
5. **Scores are whole numbers from 1 to 10.** No half points.
6. **Tension is typed once and shown in both lb and kg.** Each tension is a single number field. The setup has one lb/kg choice that covers all its tensions (mains and crosses alike), with nothing pre-selected: the reviewer must pick one. Accepted values are 30–80 lb (13.6–36.3 kg), with up to 1 decimal place. Wherever a tension is shown, both units appear: the value typed first, then its equivalent in the other unit rounded to 1 decimal place (e.g. "55 lb · 24.9 kg").
7. **Reviews and setups are entered on the site through an entry form that only logged-in reviewers can see** (role-based access):
   - The header has a **Log in** option.
   - Reviewers log in with an **email address and password**. There are three accounts, one each for Ben, Michal and Otavio, each with its own password.
   - **Log out** takes the reviewer to the home page.
   - Once logged in, the reviewer's **name in the header links to their account page**.
   - Visitors who aren't logged in only browse; they never see the entry form.
   - Once logged in, a reviewer opens a racquet's page and the entry form is available there as a tab or button, for that racquet. (Tab vs button is a design call for the team.)
   - A review is attributed to the logged-in reviewer; nobody picks a name from a list.
   - **A review can be edited only by the reviewer who wrote it**, who can change its scores and written review (the setup itself isn't editable). Nobody can edit someone else's review, and the server enforces it, not just the page.
   - **A reviewer can delete their own review, and only theirs.** Deleting it never touches anyone else's review. When the last review on a setup is deleted, the setup is removed with it; there is no separate way to delete a setup. *Why:* it fixes a setup entered under the wrong racquet by mistake, without one person's mistake costing the others their reviews. Nobody can delete someone else's review, and the server enforces it, not just the page.
   - No one edits data files by hand to add a review.
8. **Phone-first.** Design for a phone at the court first, then scale up to desktop. On a racquet's page, **Reviews come first on phones**, straight after the racquet's name, and **on desktop Reviews is its own full-width section**. At the court, the scores are what you came for.
9. **Never invent reviews, scores or reviewers.** No placeholder or sample data dressed up as real. If there's nothing yet, show an honest empty state.
10. **Racquet specs come only from official manufacturer sources.** Ben: "we will always get it from the right place", so the site doesn't record or show a source link for a racquet's specs (decided; it used to ask for one).
11. **Every racquet shows the same five specs:** head size, unstrung weight, balance, length and string pattern. **Unstrung weight is always in grams, never ounces** (Ben's decision). Other specs a manufacturer publishes (e.g. Babolat's swingweight, stiffness/RA, beam and recommended tension) aren't shown. A spec in the five that the manufacturer doesn't publish is left out, not guessed.
12. **Racquet photos are official manufacturer photos**, all shown at the same scale and on a white background (Ben's decision). The angle can differ between brands: Yonex's official shots are turned slightly, while Babolat's are flat face-on, for example.
13. **The racquet line-up is Ben's choice.** Today it's:
   - Yonex EZONE 98 (2022, 7th gen)
   - Yonex VCORE 98 (2023, 7th gen)
   - Yonex PERCEPT 97 (2023)
   - Babolat Pure Aero 98 (2023), Otavio's racquet

   Wherever racquets are listed, they're grouped into a section per brand, in this order: **Yonex, Babolat, Head, Tecnifibre, Wilson**. Every brand gets its section, even with no racquets yet, and an empty brand shows an honest "No racquets yet".
14. **Strings.** Each string has a brand, a name and a type. The type is required and comes from a fixed list: polyester, co-polyester, multifilament, synthetic gut, natural gut, Kevlar/aramid. Wherever a type is shown (lists, cards, headings, selects, screen-reader text) its label starts with a capital letter (Ben's decision, "Other decisions by Ben"): Polyester, Co-polyester, Multifilament, Synthetic gut, Natural gut, Kevlar/aramid. Only the label changes; the stored keys stay lowercase (`polyester`, `co-polyester`, `synthetic-gut`...).
   - The list starts with these ten, named as the manufacturer writes them, with the manufacturer's own type:
     - Yonex (polyester): POLYTOUR PRO, POLYTOUR FIRE, POLYTOUR DRIVE, POLYTOUR REV, POLYTOUR SPIN
     - Solinco (co-polyester): Hyper-G, Hyper-G Soft, Confidential, Confidential Soft, Mach-10
   - When entering a setup, a reviewer picks each string from the list, or chooses **Add new string** and enters its brand, name and type. It's then on the list for everyone.
   - Any logged-in reviewer can edit a string's brand, name or type (e.g. to fix spelling), and the change shows on every setup that uses it.
   - A string can be deleted only while no setup uses it.

## Theme

### Ben's decision

**Synthwave on every page, now and in the future:** retro neon purple, pink and blue on a dark background. It must stay consistent across the whole site. A new page that doesn't look like the rest of the site is a bug.

**Reviewer colours:** Ben pink, Michal cyan, Otavio purple. A colour is always shown with the reviewer's name, so colour is never the only cue.

**Scores appear as one shared score chip**, built before the score grid and used wherever a score is shown. *Why:* shared pieces keep the theme consistent across pages; copying styles page by page makes them drift.

### Team conventions

1. **Colours only via the semantic tokens** in `src/app/globals.css` (`--color-*`, `--glow-*`, `--text-glow-*`). No raw hex or `rgb()` in components or CSS Modules, and no reaching into the `--palette-*` layer directly. The palette sits under the semantic tokens so the whole site can be re-tuned in one block.
2. **Use a token where one exists** for other design values too: fonts (`--font-display`, `--font-body`), type sizes (`--font-size-*`), spacing (`--space-*`), radii (`--radius-*`), `--content-width`, focus ring and motion durations.
3. **Contrast:** text and accent tokens meet WCAG AA (4.5:1) against `--color-bg` and `--color-surface`. The measured ratios are in the header comment of `globals.css`. A new colour token needs its ratio checked and recorded there.
4. **Breakpoints.** CSS custom properties can't be used inside `@media`, so the scale is written down here. Styles are mobile-first (`min-width` queries). The code uses exactly two:

   | Breakpoint | Used for |
   | --- | --- |
   | `48rem` (768px) | wider page padding (`layout.module.css`), the landing page's four entry cards go from one column to three, with the Tennis Quiz centred on a row of its own, and the hero's top band swaps from title-then-login to login-then-title (`page.module.css`), racquet cards go to 3 columns (`racquet-list.module.css`), more space between brand sections (`racquets-by-brand.module.css`), site header goes from two rows (brand, then login state) to one row (`site-header.module.css`), score buttons on the entry forms go from two rows of five to one row of ten (`review-fields.module.css`), the new-setup form's Mains and Crosses sit side by side (`(inner)/account/racquets/[slug]/new/page.module.css`), a string's edit fields on `/account/strings` share one row (`(inner)/account/strings/page.module.css`), the add and edit racquet forms' spec fields go to two columns (`racquet-fields.module.css`) |
   | `56rem` (896px) | racquet page goes from one column (small photo beside the title) to full-width Reviews with the photo beside Specs (`(inner)/racquets/[slug]/page.module.css`); `Panel` padding grows (`panel.module.css`) |

   Reuse these. If a new one is really needed, add it to this table in the same change.
5. **Image `sizes` track the CSS.** The `sizes` prop passed to `RacquetPhoto` must match the photo's rendered width at each breakpoint. Change the CSS width and the `sizes` string together.
6. **`src/app/icon.svg` hard-codes hex values**, because it's a static file and can't read CSS variables. They mirror these palette tokens:

   | Hex | Token |
   | --- | --- |
   | `#0b0220` | `--palette-night-950` |
   | `#ffd23f` | `--palette-sunset-yellow` |
   | `#ff3cac` | `--palette-neon-pink` |
   | `#2de2e6` | `--palette-neon-cyan` |

   If one of those tokens changes, update `icon.svg` in the same change. `favicon.ico`, `icon.png` and `apple-icon.png` are rasterised from `icon.svg` (screenshots of the SVG in a browser at 16, 32 and 48 px for the ico, 192 and 180 px for the PNGs, the last two on the opaque night background), so if the artwork changes they must be re-rendered too. **Never write a double hyphen inside a comment in `icon.svg`, not even in a token name** (`--palette-night-950`): XML forbids it, and the browser then silently drops the whole icon. That is exactly why the first icon never showed in any browser; a changed icon must be checked as XML (a `DOMParser` with `image/svg+xml` reports no `parsererror`) and loaded as an image in a real browser.
7. **Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.** The global rule in `globals.css` switches animations off; don't override it.
8. **Reviewer colours** are the `--color-reviewer-*` tokens (Ben pink, Michal cyan, Otavio purple; `--color-reviewer-other` for a reviewer without one yet), picked by username in `src/components/reviewer-color.ts`. Show a reviewer's name with `ReviewerName`, which always pairs the colour with the name. Reviewer-coloured text never sits on `--color-surface-raised`, where pink and purple drop just below 4.5:1. Setup cards sit on `--color-bg`.
9. **The site name stays larger than any page heading** (Ben's decision, "The site name is bigger than any page heading"). The sizes live in `globals.css`: `--font-size-2xl` is the largest page heading (the racquet page's h1), `--font-size-brand` (the header's "LX Tennis" on every inner page) is always 1.2 times that, and `--font-size-hero` (the landing page's site name) is larger again, so the order is heading < brand < hero at every width. Give a page heading `--font-size-xl` or `--font-size-2xl`; a bigger h1 means raising `--font-size-2xl`, and the brand follows by itself (check the header still fits at 360px, with the long name and Log out).

## Stack and code

### Ben's decision

**Next.js App Router + TypeScript.** (The code is on Next.js 16 and React 19, and needs Node.js 20.9 or newer. This Next version differs from older training data, so read the guides in `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before writing Next code.)

**Database: SQLite.** During development it's a single local file, so the site works offline. Once hosted on Vercel (Ben's decision, below) it's Turso, hosted SQLite: `DATABASE_URL` and `DATABASE_AUTH_TOKEN` point at it, with no code change.

**Data model.** Stable ids are the keys, not slugs, and the database enforces the product rules where it can:

- `racquet`: id, unique slug (`brand-model-year`), brand (one of the five brands in Product rule 13), name, model year, optional generation, photo (a path or a URL, with its alt text), the five specs (each optional, since a spec the manufacturer doesn't publish is left out), and who added it. The four built-in racquets and every racquet a reviewer adds are rows of this one table.
- `string`: brand, name and type (one of the types in Product rule 14), unique by brand and name. A string that a setup uses can't be deleted.
- `setup`: its racquet, date strung, its tension unit (lb or kg), and a string, gauge and tension for both the mains and the crosses.
  - Full bed or hybrid isn't stored: a setup is a full bed when its mains and crosses have the same string and gauge. That way the same stringing can't be entered once as each.
  - Each tension is stored as the number typed; the database calculates it in kg to 1 decimal place from that number and the unit, so the two can never disagree.
  - A duplicate setup is refused (Product rule 2), comparing tensions by that kg value, so 55 lb and 24.9 kg count as the same, and so do 54.9 lb and 55 lb (both 24.9 kg).
- `review`: its setup and reviewer, unique together; the five scores as required whole-number columns checked to be 1–10 (so adding a criterion means a migration); an optional written review.

*Why:* the database itself enforces one review per person per setup, all five scores, valid score ranges and no duplicate setups, and renaming a slug can't break links between records.

**Freshness: a page is refreshed when something is saved.** Racquet pages, reviews included, stay ready-made and are served instantly. Saving or editing a setup, review or string, or deleting a review or string, refreshes the racquet pages (and the string results pages) it affects straight away (adding, editing or deleting a racquet refreshes the racquet lists and that racquet's page, which stops existing when it's deleted), so a reviewer sees their change right after saving, and so does everyone else from then on. The refresh lives in the save code in `src/data`, not on pages, so a new way of changing data can't forget it. *Why:* fastest for visitors at the court, with no stale window after a save.

### Team conventions

- **CSS Modules plus `globals.css`. No Tailwind.** One `*.module.css` file next to the component or page it styles.
- **Every page except the landing page lives in the `src/app/(inner)` route group.** Its layout wraps each page in `InnerShell` (`SiteHeader` plus the page's one `<main id="main">`), so pages there render neither. The landing page renders its own hero `<header>` and `<main id="main">`. The root `not-found.tsx` can't sit in a route group, so it uses `InnerShell` directly. *Why:* a page can't forget the header or the skip link's target.
- **Build with the shared pieces in `src/components`** instead of restyling per page: `Panel` for any card or content block (with `href` it's a whole-card link), `SectionTitle` for the pink section heading, `Disclosure` for a form or other content the reader only sometimes needs, collapsed behind its heading (a server component over a native `<details>`, so it works before JavaScript; pass `open` when it has something to show, such as a form's message), `Button` for buttons and button-like links (`primary` filled pink for a form's main action, `secondary` cyan outline otherwise; with `href` it's a link; `disabled` looks unavailable and always sits beside its reason), `BackLink` for the "← somewhere" link at the top of a page, `RacquetsByBrand` wherever racquets are listed (it renders `RacquetList`, the racquet cards, per brand; the cards go to the racquet's page unless `hrefFor` and `cta` say otherwise, as on the Add a review chooser), `EmptyState` for every "nothing here yet" (`compact` for a one-line slot in a list), `AuthStatus` for the login state in a header, `LogOutButton` wherever Log out appears. For setups and scores: `ScoreChip` wherever a score is shown (Ben's decision; a parent tints it through `--score-chip-color`, `--score-chip-fill` and `--score-chip-text`), `ReviewerName` wherever a reviewer's name goes with their colour, `SetupCard` for a setup with its grid and written reviews (built from `SetupSummary` and `ScoreGrid`, which are also used on their own), `AddReviewButton` for the logged-in reviewer's "Add your review" (on a racquet's page it swaps in `EditReviewLink`, "Edit your review", for a setup they've reviewed). For forms: `Field` (label above control) with `Input`, `Select` or `TextArea` for every field, `ReviewFields` for the five scores and the written review, `SubmitButton` for a form's main submit (pending label), `FormActions` for the Save + Cancel row at the foot of a review form (two equal columns, as wide as the fields above; give both buttons `fill`), `FormMessage` for the result of a submit read from a URL flag, `ConfirmDelete` for any delete (a `<details>` that opens to what deleting does and the real button), `RacquetFields` for the body shared by the add and edit racquet forms. Tighten a panel's padding or change its background by setting `--panel-padding` or `--panel-background` (setup cards set the background to `--color-bg`), not by overriding `padding` or `background`: two CSS Modules setting the same property would depend on which stylesheet loads last. *Why:* copies drift, and the theme has to look the same on every page.
- **Server components by default.** Add `'use client'` only where a component really needs the browser. Today: `LoginLink` and `AccountLink` (need the current path), `SubmitButton` (pending label), `FormMessage` (moves focus to itself), on the login page `LoginStatus` (moves focus to the first field), and on the new-setup page `SetupFormConstraints`, `TensionHint` and `TodayButton` (rendered only once JavaScript has loaded, so the form works without it; see the forms convention below), and in the add and edit racquet forms `RacquetFormConstraints` and `ClearFileButton` (the same idea; they live in `src/components` because both forms use them).
- **Forms work before JavaScript loads, and JavaScript only adds to them.**
  - What a form shows or hides (Full bed / Hybrid, "Add new string…") is decided by CSS on the form's own state (`:has(:checked)`), so the reveals work before any script runs.
  - A hidden field must not be `required`, or the browser blocks the submit with an error nobody can see. Fields required only while shown carry `data-required-when-shown`; once JavaScript loads, `SetupFormConstraints` sets `required` from whether they're visible, so CSS stays the one place that decides. Until then, the server's checks catch them.
  - Use HTML's own checks (`required`, `min`, `max`, `step`, `maxLength`) with the same limits as the server (`src/data/units.ts`, `src/data/limits.ts`), so most mistakes are caught before a submit reloads the page and empties the form.
  - Each URL error flag maps to a message that says what to fix and that nothing was saved, shown with `FormMessage`.
  - A choice between a few options is radios in a `fieldset` with a `legend`, not a select: one tap each. The exceptions are the string list (long, and grouped by brand), a string's type (six fixed options) and the add-a-racquet form's brand (five fixed options): selects, because that many radios would stack into a tall list on a phone. Touch targets are at least 2.75rem (44px).
- **Data only through the async accessors in `src/data`** (`racquets.ts`, `reviewers.ts`, `setups.ts`, `strings.ts`; plus `units.ts` and `limits.ts`, which are pure, with no `server-only`, so client components can use them too). Pages never reach into the raw data or the database. *Why:* data can move (local file to Turso, local disk to Vercel Blob) by rewriting accessor bodies without touching pages.
- **Freshness is built into `src/data`.** A reader whose data belongs in the static shell is `'use cache'` with `cacheLife("max")` and a `cacheTag`. Every writer in `src/data` calls `updateTag` for what it changed, after its transaction commits, so no caller can forget the refresh. The tags live in `src/data/cache-tags.ts`, next to what each one covers. A tag set inside a cached reader also expires the prerendered page that used it. `updateTag` only works inside a Server Action, so the writers are for Server Actions only.
- **The database schema and client live in `src/db`.** Only `src/data` and the tooling (`drizzle.config.ts`) import `src/db`; pages and scripts go through `src/data`. The schema is Drizzle (`src/db/schema.ts`); after changing it, run `npm run db:generate` and commit the SQL migration in `drizzle/` with it. Never edit a migration that's already been applied; add a new one.
- **Configuration comes from environment variables**, listed with comments in `.env.example`. Real values go in `.env.local`, which is gitignored, as is the local database file. Never commit a secret, a password or a database.
- **Logins and permissions are checked on the server.** `getCurrentReviewer()` (`src/auth/current-reviewer.ts`) returns the logged-in reviewer or null; any Server Action that writes data calls it and refuses when it's null. Hiding UI from visitors is only cosmetic. Changes happen through Server Actions (POST), never GET links. *Why:* anyone can call a Server Action directly, whatever the page shows.
- **Reviewers-only pages live under `/account` and are checked twice.** `src/proxy.ts` matches `/account/:path*` and sends a page load (GET/HEAD) without a valid session cookie to `/login?next=…` with a real 307 before anything renders; and the page itself calls `getCurrentReviewer()` inside the streamed part and `redirect()`s to log in when it's null, as `/account` does. The page's check is the security boundary; the proxy's is only the quick one (it can't see a password reset without the database), and it lets POSTs through because Server Actions check for themselves. *Why:* with a static shell the page has already started sending by the time it reads the cookie, so its own `redirect()` can only arrive as a `<meta>` refresh inside a 200, which is a second late and wrong for anything that isn't a browser.
- **Server Actions called from a form end in `redirect()`**, success or failure, and report errors through the URL as a flag (as `/login?failed=1` does), never what the user typed: URLs land in browser history and server logs. *Why:* a form submitted before the page's JavaScript loads is a plain POST; `redirect()` answers it with a 303 to a full page, while returning a value renders only the streamed part of the page, which shows as a blank screen.
- **Server Actions live in `src/<area>/actions.ts`** (`auth`, `racquets`, `reviews`, `strings`). The ones that write data are thin `'use server'` wrappers: `redirect(await submitX(await getCurrentReviewer(), formData))`. The work is in `src/<area>/submit.ts`: it takes the reviewer (null for a visitor, who's sent to log in before anything else is acted on; the one thing read first is the racquet slug or setup id, checked for shape, to build the page to come back to) and returns the URL to redirect to. Form fields are checked with the helpers in `src/forms/fields.ts`: every field is `unknown` until checked, and everything the database checks is checked first, with a clear error code. *Why:* the submit functions can be tested as a visitor or a reviewer without a Next request, and `redirect()` stays in the one place Next needs it.
- **Keep pages in the static shell.** Cache Components is on (`next.config.ts`), so each page is prerendered and only what reads the request (cookies, `searchParams`) renders per request. Put that read in the smallest component you can and wrap it in `<Suspense>`, as `AuthStatus` and the login page's `?next=` and error message do. Keep the space it will fill reserved, so the page doesn't jump when it streams in. There are two exceptions. The first is a setup's "Add your review" (`AddReviewButton`, which shows "Edit your review" in its place for a setup the reviewer has already reviewed), at the foot of each card: nothing is reserved, so for a logged-in reviewer the content below a card moves down by a button's height when it streams in after a link click (a hard load is held back, see below). The second is `/account` after a submit: the Email or Change password dropdown that has a result streams in open, so it grows by the form's height (a closed one doesn't move). **A logged-in reviewer's page is shown in one piece on a hard load** (Ben's request, "Header name and Account link appear late"). React holds every streamed part back until 300ms after the first paint (hard-coded in react-dom's streaming runtime, no setting), however fast the server is, so the name and buttons used to pop in about a third of a second after the page. `SessionGateScript` (in the root layout's `<head>`; `src/components/session-gate.tsx`) keeps the page invisible, in place, until the `SessionMarker` that `AuthStatus` renders is revealed (the other streamed parts are revealed in the same batch), then lifts the gate before the browser paints, so everything shows in one frame. It applies only when the readable `lx_member` hint cookie says a reviewer is logged in, so visitors keep the instant static page; it gives up after 2.5s if the marker doesn't arrive (the parts then pop in as before); and it does nothing without JavaScript. A new reviewer-only part needs nothing special: a plain `<Suspense>` as above is enough. A client-side link click isn't covered: the header stays, but the new page's own buttons come in about 300ms after its content, by the same React rule. *Why not one boundary around the whole page:* tried and measured; it shows nothing at all without JavaScript, turns an unknown id's 404 into a 200, and makes visitors wait too. *Why:* visitors are most of the audience, and a gap kept free on every card for a button they never see would cost a phone a button's height of empty space per setup. *Why:* a request read outside `<Suspense>` fails the build, and one high up would make the whole page wait. (The boring alternative, Cache Components off, renders every page in full per request: cheap for this site, but no static shell.) The one exception is a page with no ids to prerender, such as `/account/setups/[id]/review`: it awaits its setup's database lookup outside `<Suspense>` (`instant = false`) so an unknown id is a real 404, and only its login check, form and delete area stream in.
- **Unit tests** use Node's built-in `node:test`, run through `tsx` (`npm test`), in a `*.test.ts` file next to the code. Test rules and security checks (redirects, sessions, passwords), not wiring. A test that needs the database imports `@/data/test-database` **first**. That gives the file its own migrated database through the app's `getDb()` (never `local.db`), and stands in for `next/cache` (which only works inside Next), recording every `updateTag` in `refreshedTags`. This needs `--experimental-test-module-mocks`, which `npm test` passes, and Node 25.9 or newer.
- **TypeScript in strict mode.**

  - The photo is required, from exactly one of two sources: the reviewer uploads a file, or pastes an image link (see the next bullet). Either way it is any common image (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF or AVIF, up to 4 MB) and `src/racquets/process-image.ts` converts it to a 1000 × 1500 WebP that looks like the four built-ins: it is turned upright from its EXIF orientation, its uniform border is trimmed (the top-left pixel's colour, or transparency, within a threshold of 20 out of 255), and what is left is scaled to fit 1000 × 1410 and centred both ways on an opaque white canvas (Ben's decision, "Racquet photos keep a white background": a transparent source is flattened onto white, and the output has no alpha channel). So the racquet is 1410px tall with 45px clear above and below, whatever margin the photo had, as in the built-ins (the VCORE 98 spans x 276–723, y 45–1454); a racquet wider than the frame allows is fitted to the 1000px width instead. The background is not removed: the photo keeps whatever it has inside the trimmed box, so a coloured or grey uniform background stays as a block on the white canvas. A non-uniform background (a shadow or gradient darker than the threshold) isn't trimmed, so the racquet comes out smaller than the built-ins, and a busy background trims nothing, so the whole photo is scaled to fit. A picture with nothing left to show (blank, one flat colour, fully transparent, or trimmed down to a sliver under 100 × 20 px) is refused as `image-type`. All metadata (GPS included) is dropped. The accepted formats are one list, `IMAGE_TYPES` in `racquet-form.ts`, behind both the file input's `accept` and the server's check of what the bytes are (sharp reports AVIF as `heif`).
  - The WebP is stored in Vercel Blob (`src/racquets/image-store.ts`; a public store, reached through `BLOB_STORE_ID`, which Vercel adds when the store is connected to the project, or the older `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN`) and the row holds its URL. In `next dev` with neither set the file goes to `public/images/racquets/uploads/` instead (gitignored); a production server with neither refuses to add a racquet. If the row can't be saved, the stored image is removed again.
  - **An image link instead of an upload** (`src/racquets/image-fetch.ts`). Ben asked to be able to "copy an image link from another website that has the correct photo in the correct form". The team's reading (not Ben's words): the site fetches the link on the server and stores its own copy through the same pipeline as an upload (same format list, same 4,000,000-byte limit, same 1000 × 1500 WebP, same store), so the photo never disappears if the other website removes it, and `next.config.ts` needs no new image hosts. The row holds the site's own URL, never the link. The link is never put in a URL or a log, and a failed fetch saves nothing. Because the reviewer chooses where the server connects, the fetch is guarded against server-side request forgery:
    - https only, on the default port, no user name or password in the link, no whitespace, at most 2000 characters (`image-link` otherwise).
    - The host is resolved by the site and **every** address it returns must be public: loopback, private, link-local (including the cloud metadata address 169.254.169.254), carrier-grade NAT, documentation, multicast and reserved ranges are refused, as are IPv6 addresses outside global unicast and IPv4 hidden inside IPv6 (`::ffff:`, NAT64, 6to4, Teredo). IP addresses typed into the link, in any spelling (`2130706433`, `0x7f.1`, `[::ffff:7f00:1]`), are judged the same way. The connection is then made only to those vetted addresses (the request never looks the name up again), so a DNS answer that changes in between can't redirect it.
    - At most 3 redirects, and each hop gets the same treatment: https only, its host resolved and every address checked again. A redirect to a private address, to http, or with no `Location` is refused.
    - A 5-second connect timeout and a 15-second total timeout. The body is read only up to the size limit and then dropped (a `Content-Length` over it is refused without reading). It asks for no content encoding, and refuses an encoded answer, so a compressed bomb can't outgrow the count. No cookies, authorisation or referer are sent, none are kept, and each fetch uses its own connection.
    - Every failure to get an answer (host doesn't exist, refused address, timeout, error status, too many redirects) is the one flag `image-fetch`, so the form can't be used to map the network behind the server. A too-big answer is `image-size`. Whether the bytes are a photo is decided by the same sharp check as an upload, not by the `Content-Type` the other website claims, so a page or an SVG is `image-type`.
    - Tests (`src/racquets/image-fetch.test.ts`) cover the address table, loopback, private and metadata hosts and IP spellings, a host with one private answer among public ones, redirects to private addresses, to http and in a loop, oversize by `Content-Length` and by streaming, and non-200 answers, against injected network seams so they run offline; the real resolver is tested for `localhost` and private literals.
  - Specs: see Product rules 10 and 11. A spec left blank is stored as null and left out of the page.
  - Racquets are edited and deleted on the site, by any logged-in reviewer, at `/account/racquets` (see Current state, "Racquets: data and the Server Actions"); a racquet's slug never changes. The four built-in racquets came in by migration `0006_racquets_in_database`, which keeps the official pages their specs and photos came from as comments. Never edit an applied migration; a new migration is only for what the site can't do (a new brand, a changed rule).
- **Static images live under `public/images/`**, never straight in `public/`. *Why:* files in `public/` are served from the site root, so `public/racquets/` would share URLs with the `/racquets` pages.

Seeded by migration `0003` (slugs only) and extended with the specs and photos by `0006`; `0007` then rebuilt the table without `weight_ounces` and `source_url` (and their three checks). The official pages the built-in racquets' specs came from stay as comments in `0006`.
  - `string`: brand, name (both non-blank, no leading or trailing whitespace) and type, stored as `polyester`, `co-polyester`, `multifilament`, `synthetic-gut`, `natural-gut` or `kevlar-aramid`. Unique by brand and name ignoring case, so "hyper-g" collides with "Hyper-G". Seeded with Ben's ten. The stored keys stay lowercase; `STRING_TYPES` in `src/data/strings.ts` maps each to its label with a capital first letter ("Polyester", "Co-polyester", "Multifilament", "Synthetic gut", "Natural gut", "Kevlar/aramid"), and `stringTypeLabel` is the one way pages turn a key into a label. A test pins that every stored type has exactly one label and each label starts with a capital.

    - Racquet, `strung_on` (a real `YYYY-MM-DD` date), `tension_unit` (`lb` or `kg`), who created it and when.
    - Mains and crosses each have a string, gauge and tension, all required. There's no full bed/hybrid column: a setup is a full bed when its mains and crosses have the same string and gauge.
    - Numbers are exact integers. Gauge is in hundredths of a mm (1.00–1.50 mm, stored 100–150). Tension is in tenths of the setup's unit, as typed (30–80 lb or 13.6–36.3 kg, stored 300–800 or 136–363).
    - The database generates each tension in tenths of a kg (`*_tension_tenths_kg`; 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, rounded half up).
    - A duplicate is refused by one unique index on racquet, date, and the mains' and crosses' strings, gauges and kg tensions.

- **Racquets: data and the Server Actions.**
  - `createRacquet` (`src/data/racquets.ts`) inserts the row in a write transaction that first checks that no other racquet has the slug or the same brand, model and year (the duplicate rule below), so two reviewers adding the same racquet at once can't both win, then refreshes the `racquets` tag and that slug's `setups:<slug>` tag.
  - `addRacquet` (`src/racquets/actions.ts`, work in `src/racquets/submit.ts`) checks in this order, and the first failure goes back to `/account/racquets/new?error=<flag>` with nothing saved: the brand, model, year and each spec; then the photo source (exactly one of file and link: neither is `image-missing`, both is `image-both`, a link that isn't a plain https address is `image-link`, a file over the size limit is `image-size`); then that no other racquet has this brand, model and year (so a duplicate never triggers a request to another website); then, for a link, the fetch (`image-fetch`, or `image-size` when it serves too much); then that the bytes convert (`image-type`); then the image is stored; then the row is inserted. A row that can't be inserted after the image was stored (a race on the slug) removes the image again. Success goes to `/racquets/<slug>`. Visitors are sent to `/login?next=%2Faccount%2Fracquets%2Fnew` before anything else is read.

Their meaning is documented in `src/racquets/racquet-form.ts`.

  - **Duplicates.** The table enforces only a unique slug, so the rule for "the same racquet" is in `src/data/racquets.ts`, checked inside the write transaction of both adding and editing: another racquet already has the same brand, model and year, compared as the slug is (capitals, accents and punctuation don't count), or already has the slug the new one would get. Both are needed because a racquet keeps its slug when edited: after "Yonex X 2022" is renamed, its old address stays taken, and its new name is taken too. The racquet being edited never counts against itself.
  - `updateRacquet` and `deleteRacquet` (`src/racquets/actions.ts`, work in `submitRacquetUpdate` and `submitRacquetDelete` in `src/racquets/submit.ts`; the forms carry a hidden `slug`, which the server looks up, and nothing that names a reviewer):
    - **Edit** changes brand, model (the name stays the brand plus the model, as when adding), year and the five specs; a blank spec is removed. The photo is optional: neither a file nor a link keeps the current one; both is `image-both`. The slug, id, generation and author never change (the add form has no generation field, so a built-in's stays). Checks run in this order, and the first failure goes back to `/account/racquets/<slug>/edit?error=<flag>` with nothing saved: the racquet exists (else `/account/racquets?error=not-found`), the fields, the photo source, the duplicate rule (`exists`), then the same fetch, convert and store steps as adding. The row is updated in one write transaction that repeats the exists and duplicate checks. Success goes to `/account/racquets?saved=<slug>`.
    - **Photo replacement.** The new image is stored first and the old one removed only after the new row has committed, using the old `src` read in that same transaction. If the update is refused (`exists`, or the racquet was deleted meanwhile) or fails, the new image is removed again and the old one is untouched, so there is never an orphaned file and a racquet never loses its photo. A kept photo keeps its alt text, with the old "name (year)" at its start swapped for the new one if those changed; a new photo gets "name (year) racquet".
    - **Delete** removes the row only while no setup uses the racquet (one `DELETE ... WHERE NOT EXISTS (setup)` in a write transaction, with the RESTRICT foreign key as the backstop), then removes its photo. A setup using it goes back to the edit page as `?error=in-use` with nothing deleted. A racquet already gone, from a double submit, counts as done. Success goes to `/account/racquets?deleted=1`. A malformed slug goes to `?error=not-found`. The deleted racquet's page stops existing (`racquets` and `setups:<slug>` refreshed, `generateStaticParams` no longer lists it).
    - **Only files we stored are deleted** (`ImageStore.remove` in `src/racquets/image-store.ts`): Vercel Blob URLs and, in `next dev`, files under `public/images/racquets/uploads/`. The four built-in racquets' files in `public/images/racquets/` are never deleted, whether the racquet is edited or deleted; the store ignores any `src` it didn't save. A failed removal is logged, never thrown, and never hides the outcome.
    - **Freshness.** An edit refreshes only `racquets`: setup cards show no racquet details (their reader only carries the slug, which doesn't change). A delete refreshes `racquets` and `setups:<slug>`.
    - `getRacquetsByBrandWithUsage()` (cached: `racquets` and `racquet-usage`) is `getRacquetsByBrand()` with `inUse` on each racquet, so `/account/racquets` can show which ones a setup uses and can't be deleted. The server doesn't rely on it: a delete re-checks inside its own transaction.

- **URL layout.** `/setups/[id]`, alongside the existing `/racquets`, `/racquets/[slug]`, and the decided `/strings` and `/strings/[id]`. *Why:* leaves room for shareable setups.

- **Half-filled forms are kept when you leave and come back.** Ben's question: "if a user fills in half a review, leaves the page, and then comes back to the racquet it holds the data they had typed in, is that fine?" What happens (checked on a production build): moving around the site by links, Cancel or the browser's Back button keeps everything typed on the new-setup form, the review form and the Email and Change password forms (Next keeps recently visited pages hidden in the page; its docs describe this), until a reload or a typed address; a save, successful or not, empties the form. Team's view, not decided: keeping a draft of the new-setup form is a convenience on a phone at the court and is low risk (a stale date is visible; the server refuses duplicates). Not fine: (1) **Log out from the landing page doesn't clear it** (every other page's Log out does), so on a shared browser the next reviewer who logs in without reloading sees the previous person's typed draft and even their typed passwords in the Change password form; (2) the same happens when a session ends some other way; (3) **Edit your review** shows unsaved typing as if it were the saved review. Options, none built: make Log out a full page load (fixes 1 and the passwords for Log out, small); empty the password forms and the edit-review form whenever you leave them (about 10 lines in one small client component); or keep nothing on any form. Recommended: the first two, keep the drafts on the add forms.

- **Photos of added racquets and Product rule 12.** Rule 12 says racquet photos are official manufacturer photos; the form accepts any image, and can't tell. Proposal: the form says to use the official face-on shot, and the reviewer is trusted to. Ben's decision on the format (any common image, converted on upload, trimmed and scaled to match the built-ins, background not removed, put on white) is built as he gave it.

- **The 4 MB photo limit.** Vercel refuses a request body over 4.5 MB, so the form refuses photos over 4 MB, and a recent phone photo is often larger. Options, none built: resize in the browser before upload (needs JavaScript, so the form would not work without it for big photos), or upload straight to Blob from the browser (a bigger change). Today a photo between 4 MB and about 4.4 MB is refused with a message that says so; a bigger one hits Next's own request limit before the action runs and shows the generic error page instead. Either way the reviewer picks a smaller photo.

- **Product rule 13 and added racquets.** Rule 13 says the line-up is Ben's choice and lists four racquets, but any reviewer can now add a racquet. Proposal: reword it so the four are the built-in racquets, reviewers can add more, and the brand list and its order stay Ben's. Not edited here: it's Ben's rule.
